The Donkey's Golden Secret

Once upon a time, a humble donkey named Graysen lived near a deep, green forest. He was very tired of being just a plain donkey and wished he were a mighty brave king instead today.

One sunny afternoon, Graysen discovered a golden lion's skin lying abandoned in the tall grass. It was soft and shiny. Graysen thought that wearing this majestic skin would change his life in every single way.

Graysen carefully draped the heavy fur over his back. He pulled the lion’s head over his own ears. Suddenly, he looked exactly like the fiercest beast in the whole wide world, he thought right then.

Graysen marched toward the village with a proud stomp. When the little rabbits saw him coming, they scurried away in fear. They truly believed a real, hungry lion was hunting in their peaceful woods today.

Feeling very important, Graysen stood tall and puffed out his chest. He loved how everyone showed him respect. He walked through the trees, enjoying the silence that followed his every single scary step he took.

A flock of colorful birds saw the golden mane from the high branches. They chirped warnings and flew away. Graysen grinned beneath his mask, feeling like the mighty master of the beautiful green woods today.

He reached a sparkling stream and saw his reflection. The disguise was perfect. No one could see his grey ears or his donkey hooves. Graysen felt more powerful than he had ever imagined before today.

Nearby, a clever fox named Russet was resting under a shady tree. He watched the strange lion approach with curious eyes. Russet was very smart and noticed something funny about how this lion moved along.

Graysen wanted to give Russet a big scare. He crept closer, making sure the golden fur covered his legs. He prepared to let out a mighty, thunderous roar that would shake all the tall trees.

Graysen opened his mouth wide and tried his best to roar. But instead of a scary sound, a loud Hee-Haw came out. The donkey’s natural voice echoed through the trees for all to hear plainly.

Russet began to laugh so hard that he rolled on the grass. He walked right up to the disguised donkey. I might have been afraid, Russet giggled, if you had just stayed very quiet now.

You look like a lion, Russet said, wiping his eyes, but you certainly sound like a donkey. Graysen felt his long ears droop beneath the heavy mask. His secret was finally out for everyone now.

Graysen realized that pretending to be something else was not as fun as he expected. He stepped out of the heavy skin and stood tall as himself. He was just a donkey, and that's okay.

Russet smiled and walked with Graysen. It is better to be a happy donkey than a fake lion, the fox said. They walked together, enjoying the warm sun as true friends in the peaceful woods.

Moral: Clothes may disguise a fool, but his words will always give him away. It is much better to be your true self than to pretend to be someone you are not every single day.
