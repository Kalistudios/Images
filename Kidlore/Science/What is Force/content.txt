Leo and Lilly's Forceful Adventure

Lilly watched a ball roll across the green grass. "Leo, why did it move?" she asked. Leo smiled and said, "That is force! A force is just a simple push or a pull."

Leo gave his bright red toy car a big shove. "When you push things away, you use force," he explained. Lilly laughed as the car zoomed quickly across the smooth wooden playroom floor.

Next, Lilly grabbed the handle of her wooden wagon. She pulled it toward her very slowly. "I am using force too!" she cried happily. Leo nodded, watching the heavy wagon wheels turn.

Leo held a shiny red apple high in the air. He let it go, and it fell down. "Gravity is a hidden force that pulls everything toward the ground," Leo told his sister.

Lilly tried to slide her socks on the fuzzy rug. It was hard! "Friction is a force that slows things down when they rub together," Leo said, pointing at her stuck feet.

Then, Lilly stepped onto the kitchen tile. She slid easily! "Less friction here!" she shouted. Leo cheered. He explained that smooth surfaces have much less friction than rough rugs or bumpy grass.

Leo brought out two small magnets. They snapped together with a loud click. "Magnets have a special invisible force," Leo said. "They can pull metal things without even touching them at all."

Lilly tried to push the magnets together, but they pushed away! "They are fighting me!" she giggled. Leo explained that magnetic force can also push things apart if the ends are the same.

They went to the sink to play with water. Lilly pushed a rubber duck down. It popped back up! "Buoyancy is an upward force from the water," Leo said, splashing his sister.

Leo lifted a heavy box of blocks. "My muscles provide force too," he said. Lilly flexed her tiny arms. She realized that every time she moved, her muscles were using force, too.

Lilly stretched a blue rubber band. When she let go, it snapped back. "That is elastic force," Leo explained. "The band wants to return to its original shape after being pulled."

Outside, the wind caught Lilly’s bright yellow kite. "The wind is pushing the kite!" she yelled. Leo nodded. "Air can apply a strong force that moves things through the wide blue sky."

Leo dropped a tiny toy parachute from the porch. It floated slowly. "Air resistance is a force that pushes up against falling objects," Leo said. Lilly watched it drift to the grass.

Leo rubbed a balloon on his hair. He held it near Lilly. Her hair stood up! "Static electricity is a force that pulls your hair toward the balloon," Leo said with a grin.

"Forces are everywhere!" Lilly exclaimed, hugging her brother tightly. Leo hugged her back. They knew that whether pushing, pulling, or falling, forces make the whole world move and dance every single day.
