Lilly and Leo Discover Reflection

Lilly was eating breakfast when she saw another tiny girl in her silver spoon. "Leo, look!" she cried. "There is a mini-me hiding inside my cereal spoon! How did she get inside there?"

Leo laughed and sat down. "That is a reflection, Lilly! Reflection happens when light hits a smooth surface and bounces back to your eyes, just like a ball bouncing off a sturdy wall."

"Let’s go to the bathroom," Leo suggested. They stood before the big, clear mirror. "The mirror is very flat and shiny, so it sends all the light back perfectly to us right now."

Lilly waved her hand. Her reflection waved back! She stuck out her pink tongue, and the mirror girl did the same thing. "She does everything I do, Leo! It is like pure magic."

"It is science!" Leo explained. "When you move, the light bouncing off you moves too. The mirror catches that light and reflects it right back, creating a twin image of your sweet face."

They walked outside to the garden pond. The water was very still. Lilly saw the blue sky and green trees looking back at her from the deep, dark surface of the calm water.

Leo tossed a small pebble into the pond. Plop! "Look, Leo! The trees are wiggly now!" Lilly shouted. The ripples moved the water, so the light bounced in many different, messy directions today.

"When the water is bumpy, the reflection breaks," Leo said. "Light needs a very smooth path to show a clear picture. If the surface is rough, the light scatters everywhere like colorful confetti."

Lilly found a shiny metal toaster in the kitchen. "Everything looks curvy here!" she giggled. "My nose looks giant!" Leo explained that curved surfaces bend the light, making the reflection look very silly indeed.

Leo grabbed a wooden block. "Why can’t I see myself in this, Leo?" Lilly asked. "Wood is too bumpy," Leo replied. "The light hits the wood and goes in every different messy direction."

They walked to the window. Lilly saw the garden outside, but she also saw her own faint face in the glass. "Glass can do both things!" Leo said. "It lets light pass through."

Leo clapped his hands near a tall wall outside. Clap-clap! "Did you hear that, Lilly? That is an echo! An echo is just a reflection of sound bouncing off a hard flat surface."

Lilly shouted "Hello!" and the wall shouted "Hello!" back. "Sound reflects just like light!" she cheered. "Everything is bouncing today, Leo! The light, the sound, and even me as I happily jump around!"

"You are right, Lilly!" Leo laughed. "Reflection is just nature’s way of showing us the world again. Whether it is in a mirror, a pond, or a shiny spoon, light always bounces back clearly."

Lilly looked in the mirror one last time and blew a kiss. Her reflection blew one back. "I love learning with you, Leo," she said. "Now let’s go find more shiny wonderful things today!"
