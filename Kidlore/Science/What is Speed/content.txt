Lilly and Leo’s Big Book of Speed

Lilly sat in the garden grass, watching a tiny snail crawl slowly. Leo walked over with a big book. "Why is he so slow, Leo?" she asked, pointing at the sticky, shimmering trail.

Leo smiled and sat down beside his curious sister. "That is because of speed, Lilly! Speed tells us how fast something moves from one place to another over a short or long time."

"Imagine we are going to the park," Leo said. "If we walk, it takes a long time. If we run, we get there much faster! That is having more speed, you see."

"Let’s try it!" Lilly shouted. She jumped up and sprinted toward the tall oak tree. Leo followed her, laughing. Lilly reached the tree first because she used a lot of her speed.

Later, Leo hopped onto his shiny blue bicycle. He pedaled very hard. "Look at me go!" he yelled. Bicycles have wheels, which help humans move with much more speed than just running.

They walked to the bridge to watch the cars. "Cars go even faster than bikes," Leo explained. "They use powerful engines to travel many miles in just a very few minutes."

Suddenly, a loud whistle blew! A long silver train zoomed along the tracks below them. "Trains have incredible speed," Leo shouted over the noise. "They carry people across the whole country fast."

Lilly looked up and saw a white streak in the blue sky. "Is that a bird?" she asked. "No," Leo replied. "That is an airplane. It has amazing speed to fly high."

"The fastest thing made by people is a rocket," Leo said, showing a picture. "Rockets need extreme speed to leave Earth and fly all the way into the dark, quiet, starry space."

Lilly moved her arms very slowly like a green sea turtle. "And some things have very little speed," she giggled. "Like me when I am tired and do not want to walk."

"Exactly!" Leo laughed. "In nature, the cheetah is the king of speed. It can run as fast as a car on the highway to catch its lunch in the tall, yellow grass."

"Speed is everywhere!" Lilly exclaimed, giving Leo a big squeeze. "It helps us move, travel, and explore. Thank you for teaching me, Leo. Now, let’s race back home for a yummy snack!"
