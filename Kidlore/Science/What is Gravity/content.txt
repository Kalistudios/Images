The Magic Pull of Gravity

Lilly held her bright red ball high in the air. She let it go, and it fell quickly to the grass. "Why does it always fall down, Leo?" she asked her brother.

Leo smiled and adjusted his glasses. "That is because of gravity, Lilly. Gravity is an invisible force that pulls everything toward the center of the Earth. It never ever stops its strong pull."

Lilly tried to jump as high as she could. She soared into the air for a tiny second, but she landed right back on the soft green grass. "I cannot stay up!"

"Exactly!" Leo said, pointing at the ground. "Without gravity, we would all float away into the dark sky. Even the houses, the trees, and the blue oceans would drift far away today."

Lilly looked at a bird flying high above them. "Does gravity pull the birds too?" she asked. Leo nodded. "Yes, but their wings push against the air to stay up very high."

Leo picked up a small, round stone from the garden path. He held it out for Lilly to see. "Everything with mass has gravity. The bigger something is, the stronger it pulls."

"Is the Earth very big then?" Lilly asked, her eyes wide. She looked down at her small shoes. "Yes," Leo answered. "Earth is giant, so it has a very powerful, constant pull."

Leo pointed toward the pale moon in the afternoon sky. "The moon has gravity too, but it is smaller than Earth. You could jump much higher and stay up longer there."

Lilly imagined herself bouncing on the moon like a fluffy bunny. "Would I be lighter there?" she asked. Leo laughed softly. "Yes, you would feel very light because the pull is weaker."

Leo grabbed a shiny green apple from the kitchen basket. He let it drop onto the table with a thud. "A famous scientist named Newton once watched an apple fall just like this."

Lilly poured some cold juice into a tall glass. The liquid stayed at the bottom. "Gravity keeps my juice in the cup!" she shouted happily. She loved learning about this invisible force.

Leo gave his little sister a big, warm hug. "Gravity keeps us safe on the ground together," he said. Lilly smiled, happy to be right where she was, held by the Earth.
