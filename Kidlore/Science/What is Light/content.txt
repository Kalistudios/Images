The Magic of Light

Lilly woke up early and saw bright beams dancing on her soft bedroom floor. She called for her brother, Leo. "What is this bright stuff everywhere?" she asked, pointing at the golden morning sun.

Leo smiled and knelt down beside her. "That is light, Lilly," he explained gently. "Light is a special energy that moves very fast, even faster than a racing car or a zooming jet."

Lilly jumped up and started dancing. She noticed a dark shape following her every move. "Look, Leo! A shadow monster!" she shouted. Leo laughed. "A shadow is just where light cannot go through."

They went to the bathroom mirror. Leo held a shiny silver spoon. "Light bounces off things," he said. Lilly saw her nose look very big. "It is called a reflection," Leo told her.

Next, they looked at a glass of water. Lilly put a red straw inside. "The straw looks broken!" she cried. Leo explained, "Light bends when it travels through water. This is called refraction."

They walked outside to the garden. A beautiful rainbow stretched across the blue sky. "The white sunlight hits raindrops and splits into many colors," Leo said. Lilly counted all seven of the colors.

Lilly touched a green leaf. "Does the sun help the flowers?" she asked. Leo nodded. "Yes, plants use light to make their own food. Light helps everything in nature grow big and strong."

Inside, Leo flipped a switch. A bright bulb filled the room with glow. "The sun gives us light, but we can make it too," Leo said. Lilly clapped her hands at the lamp.

The sun started to set. The sky turned pink and orange. "Where does the light go?" Lilly asked sadly. Leo pointed to the horizon. "It is just shining on the other side now."

Soon, tiny fireflies began to blink in the dark bushes. Lilly tried to catch one. "Look, Leo! These bugs carry their own little lamps!" she whispered. "That is called bioluminescence," Leo whispered back.

They looked up at the twinkling stars. "The stars are very far away," Leo said. "Their light travels for a long time to reach us. We are seeing light from very long ago."

Leo tucked Lilly into bed and turned on her small nightlight. "Light keeps us safe and helps us see the world," he said. Lilly yawned. "Thank you for teaching me about light, Leo."
