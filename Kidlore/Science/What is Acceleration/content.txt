Lilly and Leo’s Fast Adventure

Lilly watched Leo pedal his shiny bike. "Why are you going faster and faster?" she asked curiously. Leo stopped and smiled. "That is called acceleration, Lilly! It means my speed is changing."

"When I start pedaling, I am speeding up," Leo explained. Lilly hopped on her small scooter. She pushed off the ground and began to move. "Look at me, I am accelerating too!"

Leo zoomed down the sidewalk. "The harder I push, the faster I go!" he shouted. Lilly giggled as she tried to keep up. Speeding up is the most common type of acceleration.

"Wait, I need to stop!" Lilly cried as she saw a fuzzy caterpillar. She squeezed her brakes. Leo slowed down beside her. "Slowing down is also a kind of acceleration," he said.

Leo explained that scientists call slowing down 'deceleration.' "Whenever your speed changes, even if you are getting slower, you are still accelerating," he added kindly. Lilly nodded while watching the tiny caterpillar crawl.

"There is one more secret type," Leo said, pointing toward the curved path ahead. "Acceleration also happens when you change your direction." Lilly looked puzzled. "Even if I stay at one speed?"

Leo steered his bike around a sharp corner. Lilly followed him on her scooter. "Yes! Because you are changing which way you go, your velocity changes. That is acceleration too," Leo explained.

They reached a straight part of the path. They both moved at a steady, calm pace. "Are we accelerating now?" Lilly asked. Leo shook his head. "No, we are moving at constant speed."

Suddenly, Leo did a quick zig-zag! "Now I am!" he laughed. Lilly tried to copy him, wiggling her scooter back and forth. Changing direction quickly is a very fun way to accelerate.

They walked to the park’s tall slide. Leo dropped a ball from the top. It moved faster as it fell. "Gravity makes things accelerate downward toward the ground," Leo told his sister.

Lilly climbed onto the blue swing. Leo gave her a big push. As she went up and down, she changed speed and direction. "I am accelerating the whole time!" she cheered loudly.

Leo threw a frisbee into the air. It curved through the sky and slowed down before landing. "The frisbee accelerated when I threw it and when it changed its path," Leo said.

"So," Lilly summarized, "acceleration is speeding up, slowing down, or turning a corner." Leo clapped his hands. "You got it! It is all about any change in how you are moving along."

They saw a car drive past and a bird fly high above. "Everything that moves and changes is accelerating," Leo said. Lilly felt very smart knowing the secret of the moving world.

Lilly and Leo raced back home together. "I love acceleration!" Lilly shouted happily. They knew that whether they were zooming, braking, or turning, science was making their afternoon a very fast adventure.
