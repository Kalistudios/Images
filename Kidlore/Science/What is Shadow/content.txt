Lilly, Leo, and the Shadow Mystery

Lilly was playing outside when she saw a dark shape following her. She jumped, but the dark shape jumped too! "Leo, what is this dark monster on the grass?" she asked very loudly.

Leo laughed and walked over to his little sister. "That is not a monster, Lilly! That is just your shadow," he said gently. "It follows you because you are blocking the bright sun."

Lilly looked down at the dark shape. It looked just like her! "But how does it get there, Leo?" she asked. Leo took her hand and led her toward the big, shady oak tree.

"Shadows need two things to exist," Leo explained, holding up two fingers. "First, you need a bright light, like the sun. Second, you need something solid to block that bright, yellow light today."

Lilly stood in a beam of sunlight. "I am blocking the light!" she cheered. Behind her, a perfect dark outline appeared on the pavement. She moved her arms and the shadow moved too.

"Light travels in straight lines," Leo said, using a flashlight to show her. "It cannot bend around you. When your body gets in the way, the light stops and creates a dark space."

Lilly put her hands together to make a bird shape. On the wall, a dark bird flapped its wings! "Look, Leo! I made a bird out of nothing but my hands and light!"

"See how the shadow changes?" Leo asked. He moved the flashlight closer to Lilly's hands. The shadow bird grew very large and spooky on the bedroom wall. Lilly gasped at the big bird.

"When you are close to the light, you block more of it," Lilly noticed. She moved her hands closer to the lamp, making the shadow take up the whole side of the room.

Leo moved the light further away. The shadow became small and sharp. "The further you are from the light, the smaller your shadow stays. It is like a fun game of hide-and-seek."

Lilly pointed at the long shadows on the grass. The sun was going down. "Why is my shadow so long now, Leo? It looks like I have very tall giant legs today!"

"The sun is low in the sky," Leo explained. "When light hits you from the side, it stretches your shadow out. At noon, when the sun is above, your shadow is very short."

That night, Lilly saw shadows on her wall from the moon. "Even the moon makes shadows, Leo!" she whispered. The trees outside cast dancing patterns across her cozy, warm blankets in bed.

"Shadows are just parts of the world where light cannot reach," Leo said, tucking her in. "They are not scary. They are just proof that there is a bright light shining somewhere nearby."

Lilly smiled and closed her eyes. She wasn't afraid of the dark shapes anymore. With Leo’s help, she knew that shadows were just a fun part of the light’s magical and mysterious dance.
