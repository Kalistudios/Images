Corvus and the Clever Plan

Corvus the clever crow soared across the scorched, golden desert. The sun blazed high in the sky, and the air was very hot. His throat felt dry and scratchy from the heat.

He looked down at the dusty ground, hoping to find a cool, splashing stream. But every river had dried up, leaving only cracked mud behind. Corvus needed to find water very soon.

Suddenly, his sharp eyes spotted something shimmering near an old stone wall. It was a tall, white pitcher tucked away in the shade. Corvus flapped his wings and landed right beside it.

He peered over the narrow rim of the pitcher. Deep down at the very bottom, he saw a small pool of clear water. It looked so refreshing and cool to the touch.

Corvus stretched his neck as far as he could. He reached his long, dark beak down, but the water was too far away. No matter how hard he tried, he could not drink.

He pushed against the heavy pitcher with his strong chest. He hoped to tip it over so the water would spill out. But the pitcher was made of heavy stone and stayed still.

Corvus sat on the ground, feeling very tired and discouraged. He looked at the dry ground and sighed. Then, he noticed a pile of small, smooth pebbles glowing in the bright sunlight.

A brilliant idea popped into his clever mind! He hopped over and picked up a tiny pebble in his beak. It was heavy, but Corvus was determined to make his plan work.

He flew back to the top of the pitcher and dropped the pebble inside. Plink! The water at the bottom rippled, and it rose just a tiny bit higher than before.

Corvus did not stop there. He picked up another pebble, and then another. One by one, he dropped them into the pitcher. He worked very hard under the hot, afternoon sun.

Slowly but surely, the water rose higher and higher with every stone. Finally, the cool water reached the very top of the pitcher. Corvus let out a happy, loud "Caw!" of joy.

Corvus drank the sweet, cool water until he was no longer thirsty. He learned that with patience and clever thinking, even the hardest problems can be solved. He flew home very happy.
