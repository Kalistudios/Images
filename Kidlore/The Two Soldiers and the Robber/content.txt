The Brave Soldier and the Boastful Friend

Valerius and Gaius were two soldiers traveling together along a very dusty road. Valerius was strong and carried a heavy shield, while Gaius walked beside him, talking about his own great courage.

The sun was hot as they moved through a dark and tangled forest. The trees were tall, and the path became very narrow. Both men gripped their swords tightly, watching for any danger.

Suddenly, a fierce robber named Drako jumped out from behind a large rock. He held a jagged blade and looked very mean. Drako growled loudly at the two soldiers, blocking their narrow path.

Drako pointed his weapon at Valerius and demanded all their gold coins. He looked ready to fight both of them. Valerius stepped forward bravely, raising his heavy shield to protect his frightened friend.

Gaius saw the sharp blade and the robber's angry face. His knees began to shake, and his face turned pale with fear. He forgot all his earlier boasts about being a brave soldier.

Instead of helping his partner, Gaius turned around and ran into the deep woods as fast as he could. He hid behind a thick tree, leaving Valerius all alone to face Drako.

Valerius did not run away like his friend. He drew his sword and stood firm against the robber. He knew he had to defend himself, even if he had to fight the battle alone.

Drako attacked with great force, but Valerius was very skilled. They clashed in the middle of the road. Valerius used his shield to block every blow while looking for a way to win.

With a mighty shout, Valerius swung his sword and struck the robber’s weapon right out of his hand. Drako gasped in surprise, realizing that he had finally met a very powerful opponent today.

Terrified by the soldier's strength, Drako turned and fled into the dark forest. Valerius lowered his sword and took a deep breath. He was safe, and the danger was finally gone for now.

Once the road was quiet, Gaius slowly crept out from his hiding place behind the tree. He looked around carefully to make sure the robber was gone before stepping back onto the path.

Suddenly, Gaius pulled out his sword and began waving it in the air. He made a very fierce face and shouted loudly into the empty woods, acting as if he were very brave.

"Where is that thief?" Gaius cried. "Let him come back so I can teach him a lesson! I would have cut him down if I had only been just a little bit faster."

Valerius looked at Gaius with a sad smile. "I wish you had helped me when it mattered," he said. "Your words are brave now, but your feet were very fast when Drako attacked."

The moral of the story is that it is easy to act brave after the danger has passed. A true friend shows their courage when you really need help, not just later.
