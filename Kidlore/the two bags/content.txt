The Traveler and His Two Bags

Once upon a time, a young traveler named Sagos began a very long journey across the wide world. He wore a sturdy tunic and held a walking stick as he stepped onto the path.

As Sagos walked, he carried two magical bags given to him by the Great Spirit. One bag hung right in front of his chest, while the other was tied tightly behind his back.

The bag in the front was easy for Sagos to see. It was bright yellow and filled with heavy stones. Every time he looked down, he could inspect every single stone inside it.

These stones represented the mistakes and faults of every person Sagos met. Whenever someone was loud or messy, a new stone appeared in his front bag for him to see and judge.

Sagos spent all day staring at the faults of his friends. He noticed when they were lazy or when they forgot their manners. The front bag was always right under his very nose.

However, there was another bag tucked away where Sagos could never see it. This bag was dark blue and hung heavy behind his shoulders, resting right against his spine as he walked along.

Inside this hidden back bag were his own mistakes. Every time Sagos was grumpy or unkind, a heavy stone was added to it. But because it was behind him, he never noticed.

The bag on his back grew heavier and heavier every single day. Sagos felt his legs getting tired, but he did not know why. He only looked at the yellow bag in front.

While walking through a sunny village, Sagos pointed at a neighbor who had spilled some milk. "Look at your messy mistake!" he shouted, watching a stone drop into his bright front bag.

Sagos felt very wise because he could see everyone else's problems so clearly. He thought he was perfect because his eyes never landed on the dark blue bag that stayed hidden behind him.

Suddenly, the weight of the back bag became too much to bear. Sagos stumbled on a small pebble and fell forward. The hidden bag swung around, hitting the ground with a loud thud.

"What is this?" Sagos cried out in surprise. He reached behind his shoulders and pulled the dark blue bag forward. He gasped when he saw how many large, heavy stones were inside it.

He realized that while he was busy judging others, he had ignored his own faults. The back bag was twice as full as the front one, filled with mistakes he had completely forgotten.

Sagos decided to turn his bags around. He wanted to keep his own faults where he could see them. He learned that understanding himself was much more important than judging his many friends.

The moral of the story is that we are often quick to see the faults of others, but we remain blind to our own mistakes. We must look inward to grow better.
