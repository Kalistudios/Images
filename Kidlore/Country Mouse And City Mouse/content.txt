Silas and the City Lights

Silas lived in a quiet, green field. He loved the smell of fresh grass and the warm sun. He spent his days collecting seeds and drinking cold water from the clear, blue stream.

For dinner, Silas ate hard corn and crunchy roots. It was a humble meal, but he was always safe and happy. He enjoyed the stars and the soft sounds of the quiet night.

One sunny morning, his cousin Spire arrived from the city. Spire wore a tiny blue vest and a shiny hat. He looked around the dusty field and gave a very long, loud sigh.

"This food is too plain," Spire complained. "The city has cakes and cheese! You must come with me to see how grand life can be." Silas agreed to go on a big adventure.

They traveled a long way to reach the noisy city. The buildings were very tall, and the lights were very bright. Silas felt tiny as loud, metal cars zoomed past them quickly today.

Spire led Silas inside a giant, fancy house. "Welcome to my home!" Spire said proudly. The rooms were filled with soft carpets and golden furniture. It looked like a palace for two mice.

They climbed onto a big table covered in leftovers. There were sweet tarts, creamy cheese, and crusty bread. Silas had never seen such a feast. He began to eat a piece of cake.

Suddenly, the door creaked open. A huge, orange cat with sharp claws crept into the room. Its yellow eyes glowed. Silas froze, his heart beating fast with a whole lot of scary fear.

"Run!" Spire squeaked. They jumped off the table and scrambled into a dark hole in the wall. Silas trembled as he heard the cat scratching at the wood outside their small hiding spot.

Later, they tried to eat again, but a loud dog started barking. A human walked in and turned on the lights. Silas had to hide once more, feeling very scared and extremely hungry.

Silas looked at his cousin. "You have fancy food, but you are always in danger," he said. "I prefer my quiet home and my simple roots. I am going back to the country."

Silas returned to his peaceful field. He ate his corn in the warm sun, feeling very lucky. He learned that a simple life with safety is much better than a rich life today.
