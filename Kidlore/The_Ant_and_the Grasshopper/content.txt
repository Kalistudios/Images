The Song of the Seasons

Lior was a very tiny ant with a very big heart for work. Every single morning, he marched through the tall green grass, looking for seeds to save for the cold winter. He stayed very busy.

Nearby, Ziv the grasshopper lived a very different life. He spent his sunny days playing a golden fiddle and singing happy songs. To Ziv, every day was meant for dancing and fun. Life was just grand.

The sun beat down on the meadow, but Lior did not stop. He found a golden kernel of corn that was twice his size. He pushed and pulled it toward his home with all his might.

"Hey there, Lior!" Ziv called out, pausing his music. "The sun is shining and the breeze is sweet. Why are you working so hard today? Come and dance in the warm, yellow, and lovely bright light!"

Lior wiped his brow and looked up. "I am storing food for the winter," he explained softly. "When the snow falls, there will be nothing to eat. You should start working, too. Please listen to me."

Ziv just laughed and hopped away. "Winter is a long way off, my friend! Why worry about tomorrow when today is so beautiful?" He played a loud, cheerful, and very happy tune on his shiny fiddle.

Autumn arrived, and the leaves turned bright orange and red. Lior continued his chores, gathering dried berries and crunchy seeds. His underground pantry was becoming very full of delicious, healthy, and tasty little winter food treats.

The wind grew chilly, and the flowers began to fade. Ziv found it harder to sing when his toes were cold. He looked for a snack, but the meadow was now empty, brown, and very quiet.

One morning, a soft white blanket of snow covered the world. Lior was safe inside his warm house. He sat by a tiny fire, enjoying a hearty meal of corn, berries, and many small, crunchy seeds.

Outside, Ziv was freezing and hungry. He remembered Lior's warning and felt very foolish. He slowly trudged through the deep snow until he reached the small, round, brown door of the tiny and hard working ant.

Ziv knocked gently on the door. Lior opened it and saw his friend shivering in the cold. "I have no food and I am so cold," Ziv whispered. Lior felt very sad for his poor friend.

Lior pulled Ziv inside. "I have enough to share," he said. As they ate, Ziv promised that next summer, he would work first and play later. It was a lesson well learned for the future year.
