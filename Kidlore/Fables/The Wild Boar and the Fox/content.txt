The Ready Boar

Deep within the ancient green forest, Kapros the wild boar lived a very quiet life. He was a large animal with coarse brown fur and two very sharp, curved, white tusks.

One sunny morning, Kapros found a sturdy oak tree with rough bark. He began to rub his long tusks against the wood. He moved his head back and forth with great strength.

Scrape, scrape, scrape went his tusks against the tree. Kapros was working very hard to make his weapons as sharp as possible. He did not stop to rest or eat any berries.

Nearby, Alopex the fox was watching from behind a thick bush. He saw the boar working hard. Alopex wondered why the big boar was acting so busy on such a lovely day.

Alopex stepped out into the clearing and trotted toward Kapros. He had a playful smile on his face. He sat down and watched the boar continue his strange and very repetitive task.

"Good morning, Kapros!" Alopex called out loudly. "Why are you working so hard today? The sun is shining, the birds are singing, and the entire forest is very quiet and safe now."

Kapros did not stop his work. He kept rubbing his tusks against the trunk. Alopex looked around at the peaceful woods. There were no hunters or hungry wolves to be seen anywhere.

"You are wasting your time," Alopex laughed. "There is no danger near us. You should be playing or napping in the shade. Why sharpen your tusks when there is nobody to fight?"

Kapros finally stopped and looked at the fox. He took a deep breath. His tusks were now shining brightly in the sunlight. He looked very serious as he spoke to Alopex.

"My dear friend," Kapros said gently. "I sharpen my tusks now because I have the time. If a hunter or a wolf appears, I will be too busy trying to stay alive."

"When danger arrives, it is far too late to prepare," the boar explained. "I must be ready before the trouble starts. My sharp tusks are my only defense against a fierce enemy."

Alopex stopped laughing and thought about those wise words. He realized that the boar was right. Being prepared is much better than being caught by surprise when a scary moment finally happens.

Kapros turned back to the oak tree. He gave his tusks one last rub to ensure they were perfect. He felt very ready for whatever might happen in the deep, dark forest.

Alopex nodded his head and walked away quietly. He decided that he should also practice his running and hiding skills today. It was always best to be ready for any future danger.

The moral of this story is that it is best to prepare for danger before it arrives. If you wait until you are in trouble, it will be too late to get ready.
