The Mountain and the Mouse

High above the golden valley stood Orestes, the tallest mountain in the entire world. He was covered in heavy snow and sharp rocks. Every day, he watched the white clouds drift slowly by.

One morning, a deep rumble started deep within Orestes. It was a low sound that vibrated through the earth. The rocks on his steep slopes began to tremble and dance about wildly.

A puff of dark smoke rose from the very peak of Orestes. It looked like a giant grey cloud was sitting on his head. The sky grew dark as the mountain groaned loudly.

Orestes felt a huge pressure inside his stony heart. He puffed and huffed, sending sparks flying into the air. The whole world watched the great mountain, waiting for something truly massive to happen.

The ground shook so hard that the trees at the base of Orestes wobbled. Boulders rolled down his sides like giant marbles. Everyone thought a terrible fire or a giant would soon appear.

“CRACK! BOOM!” went the mountain. The sound was louder than a thousand drums beating at once. Orestes seemed to grow taller and wider as he prepared for his big, noisy, spectacular moment.

Huge cracks appeared on the face of Orestes. Bright light flickered deep inside the dark stone gaps. The mountain let out a mighty sigh that blew the clouds away across the blue sky.

Orestes strained and groaned with all his might. The mountain’s peak glowed with a strange, orange light. Something was coming out of the center of the giant rocks, and it was coming soon.

One final, enormous shake rocked the valley. Orestes gave a giant heave that moved the very stars. A cloud of thick dust exploded from a small hole near the mountain's very top peak.

Suddenly, everything went quiet. The smoke cleared away, and the rumbling stopped. Orestes stood still once more. The valley waited in silence to see what great wonder the mountain had created today.

Out of the dusty hole stepped a tiny, fuzzy creature. It had two round ears and a long, pink tail. It was Milo, a very small mouse with big, bright, curious black eyes.

Milo blinked at the bright sunlight and twitched his little nose. He did not look scary or big at all. He was just a tiny mouse looking for a small piece of cheese.

Orestes looked down at the tiny Milo sitting on his shoulder. The giant mountain seemed very surprised. All that noise and shaking had only produced one very small, very cute, and fuzzy mouse.

Milo gave a tiny squeak and began to wash his face with his paws. He was perfectly happy being small. The great mountain’s big labor had ended with a very tiny, quiet surprise.

The moral is that big promises often lead to small results. Do not be fooled by loud noises and grand displays, because true greatness is not always measured by how big you are.
