The Lion, the Fox, and the Ass

Ari the majestic Lion and Renard the clever Fox met under a baobab tree. They agreed that hunting together would be much easier than searching for a tasty meal all alone today.

Soon, Burro the honest Ass joined them. The three partners walked across the golden plains, searching for a herd. They promised to share whatever they caught during their long afternoon of hunting.

Ari used his powerful nose to track the scent of deer. He led the way with pride, while Renard followed closely behind, planning their strategy with a very sharp and cunning mind.

Burro stood near a watering hole, waiting for the signal. He was ready to use his very loud voice to scare the prey toward the hidden Lion and the waiting, clever Fox.

Renard crawled through the swaying grass, his bright eyes watching every movement. He stayed low and quiet, waiting for the perfect moment to jump out and help his two hungry forest friends.

Their plan worked perfectly, and they caught a great deal of food. Ari and Renard stood over the giant pile of meat, breathing heavily after their very long and successful morning hunt.

Ari looked at Burro and commanded him to divide the treasure. He wanted the honest Ass to make sure everyone was happy with their share of the delicious food they had caught.

Burro worked hard to make three identical piles of meat. He used his hooves to measure each portion, ensuring that Ari and Renard received the exact same amount as he did himself.

Ari looked at the three equal piles and felt a terrible rage. He believed a King deserved much more than a Fox or an Ass. He let out a truly deafening roar.

Without warning, Ari pounced on poor Burro. He punished the Ass for being too fair. Now, only Ari and Renard remained on the plains, standing beside the many piles of fresh meat.

Ari turned his golden eyes toward Renard. He told the Fox to divide the food again. Renard knew he had to be very careful if he wanted to stay safe from Ari.

Renard quickly pushed almost all the food into one enormous heap. He left only a tiny, single bone for himself. He worked fast to show the Lion that he was very humble.

Ari looked at the giant pile and smiled widely. He was very pleased with this new arrangement. He asked Renard where he had learned to be such a wise and clever animal.

Renard glanced back at what happened to Burro. He told the Lion that the fate of the Ass had been his teacher. He learned that the strongest always gets the biggest share.

We should always learn from the mistakes of others. By watching what happens to those who are foolish, we can find the wisdom to stay safe when facing a very powerful foe.
