The Peacock and the Crane

In a very lush garden, there lived a magnificent peacock named Argos. He had long feathers that shimmered with bright gold and deep blue. Argos was the proudest bird in the whole world.

Argos loved to walk slowly across the green grass. He wanted everyone to see his shiny coat. He spent many hours cleaning his feathers so they would sparkle under the bright, warm sun.

One afternoon, a plain bird landed near a small pond. This was Kōuru the Crane. Kōuru had long legs and simple, gray feathers. He looked very different from the colorful and fancy Argos.

Argos looked at Kōuru and let out a very loud laugh. He shook his head and walked toward the Crane. Argos felt very superior because he thought Kōuru looked quite boring and dull.

Argos spread his tail into a huge, round fan. The many colors flashed like tiny jewels. "Look at me!" Argos cried out loudly. "I am dressed like a king in beautiful, royal silk."

Then Argos pointed a wing at Kōuru. "Look at your feathers," he sneered. "They are as gray as the dust on the ground. You are a very plain and very ugly bird indeed."

Kōuru did not feel sad or angry. He stood very still on his long, thin legs. He looked at Argos and his bright tail. Kōuru knew that beauty was not the only thing.

Argos continued to strut around the pond. He fluffed his neck feathers and acted very grand. He thought that his beauty made him much better than any other bird in the wide forest.

"It is true," Kōuru said softly. "Your feathers are very bright. But tell me, Argos, can you fly high into the sky? Can you touch the clouds with those heavy and pretty things?"

Argos stopped dancing and looked surprised. He looked at his heavy tail. He could only hop a little bit. He could not soar high like the other birds that lived in the trees.

Kōuru stretched his large, gray wings wide. They were strong and powerful. "I may be plain," Kōuru said, "but my wings can carry me across the world to many new and exciting places."

With a great leap, Kōuru flapped his wings. He rose up from the ground quickly. He flew higher and higher until he was circling way above the tall trees and the garden walls.

From high above, Kōuru looked down at the tiny peacock. Argos looked like a small, colorful dot. Kōuru felt free and happy as he glided through the cool, fresh air of the sky.

Argos stood alone on the muddy ground. He realized that his beauty was just for show. He could not fly like Kōuru. He was stuck on the earth while Kōuru reached the stars.

The moral of this story is that it is better to be useful than to be beautiful. Plain things often have great value. We should never look down on others for being simple.
