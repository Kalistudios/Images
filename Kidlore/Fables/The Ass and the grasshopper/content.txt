Bramble and the Grasshopper's Song

Bramble the donkey lived in a bright, green meadow filled with many colorful flowers. He was a very happy donkey, but he often wished he could make a sound that was truly sweet.

One sunny afternoon, Bramble heard a wonderful, high-pitched melody floating through the warm air. It was the loveliest song he had ever heard. He stopped chewing his long grass and listened very closely today.

He followed the sound until he found a tiny, bright green insect sitting on a leaf. It was Chirp the Grasshopper. Chirp was rubbing his wings together to make a magical, musical sound.

"Oh, Chirp!" Bramble brayed loudly. "Your voice is so light and pretty. I wish I could sing just like you. My own voice is much too loud and very, very scratchy to hear."

Bramble leaned in closer to the tiny grasshopper. "Tell me your secret," he whispered softly. "What do you eat to make such music? Is it special seeds or perhaps some very sweet garden flowers?"

Chirp looked up at the big donkey and smiled kindly. "I do not eat heavy food at all," Chirp chirped. "I only drink the sparkling dew that settles on the grass every morning."

Bramble decided right then that he would change his ways. "No more dry hay for me!" he declared. "I will only drink the morning dew. Soon, I will sing like a beautiful bird."

The next morning, Bramble went out into the field. He licked the tiny drops of water from the clovers. The dew was cool and fresh, but it did not fill his hungry belly.

Days went by, and Bramble grew very thin. His legs felt shaky, and his tummy made loud, rumbling noises. He tried to sing, but only a tiny, weak "hee-haw" came out of him.

Chirp hopped over and saw his friend looking very tired. Bramble was lying in the grass, feeling quite silly. He realized that a large donkey cannot live on tiny drops of water alone.

"Bramble," Chirp said gently. "You are a strong donkey, not a tiny bug. You have your own special talents. You don't need to change who you are to be wonderful and truly special."

Bramble agreed and ate a huge pile of golden hay. He let out a giant, happy bray that echoed across the hills. He was proud to be a donkey with a loud voice.
