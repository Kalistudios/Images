The Clever Goat's High Ground

Gilda the Goat stood high upon a jagged, rocky peak. The air was cool, and the view was grand. She felt very safe up there, far away from any danger below her.

Down in the shadows of the valley, Silas the Wolf watched Gilda with hungry eyes. He licked his lips and imagined a very delicious dinner. However, the cliff was much too steep.

Silas tried to scramble up the rocks, but his paws slipped on the smooth stones. He growled in frustration. He needed a clever plan to make the goat come down to him.

Gilda peered over the edge and saw the wolf. She was not afraid because she knew he could not reach her. She continued to nibble on a tiny, lonely tuft of moss.

"Dear friend Gilda," Silas called out in his softest, kindest voice. "Why stay up there on those cold rocks? The meadow down here is full of the sweetest, greenest grass imaginable today."

Silas pointed his paw toward the rolling hills. "The sun warms this beautiful grass perfectly. It is much softer than those sharp stones. You should come down and enjoy this tasty feast."

Gilda looked down at Silas. She wondered if the wolf was being truly honest. The grass did look very bright and inviting. Her tummy gave a tiny, quiet rumble of great hunger.

Silas continued his tricky speech. "There are also sweet purple flowers blooming near the stream. They are much better than that dry moss you are eating. Come and taste them right now!"

The goat moved slightly closer to the steep ledge. She looked at the sparkling water and the lush meadow below. She was tempted by the thought of a delicious and soft meal.

Silas quickly hid behind a large, flowery bush. He crouched low to the ground and prepared to spring. He kept his tail very still and held his breath, waiting for Gilda’s jump.

Gilda watched the bush carefully. She saw a tiny flash of grey fur and a sharp, hungry eye peeking out. She realized the wolf was hiding and waiting to catch her soon.

Gilda realized that Silas did not care about her appetite at all. He only wanted her to come down so he could eat her. The wolf was a very sneaky, lying hunter.

"Thank you for the kind offer, Silas," Gilda shouted down. "But I know you are not inviting me to dinner. You are only inviting me to be your dinner! I shall stay."

Silas realized his clever trick had failed completely. He walked away into the dark woods, feeling very hungry and grumpy. Gilda watched him go, feeling proud of her own sharp, wise mind.

Gilda stayed safe on her high peak while Silas slunk away. The moral of the story is: Beware of advice from those who only seek to gain from your own terrible loss.
