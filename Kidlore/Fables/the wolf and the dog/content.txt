The Wolf and the Dog

Ulf the wolf was very hungry. It was a cold winter day in the snowy forest. He had not eaten for a long time. His ribs showed through his thin, gray fur.

Suddenly, Ulf saw Alaric the dog walking along the path. Alaric looked very healthy and strong. His coat was shiny and smooth. He was much bigger and heavier than the poor, thin wolf.

Ulf stopped to talk to the plump dog. "How do you stay so fat and happy?" he asked. "I am a fierce hunter, but I am starving while you look very well fed."

Alaric wagged his tail and smiled. "I do not hunt at all," he explained. "My master gives me plenty of food. I get meat and scraps every single day without any hard work."

Ulf's eyes went wide with wonder. "That sounds like a wonderful life," he said. "I spend all my time running and searching for food in the cold snow. I am always so tired."

"You can have this life too," Alaric said kindly. "Just come with me to my master's house. You only have to guard the yard and be friendly to the family each day."

Ulf thought this was a great idea. He began to walk with Alaric toward the village. He dreamed of warm meals and a soft place to sleep away from the biting winter wind.

As they walked, Ulf looked closely at Alaric. He noticed a strange mark on the dog's neck. The fur was rubbed away in a circle. It looked very sore and quite unusual indeed.

"What happened to your neck?" Ulf asked. "The fur is gone around your throat. Did you get into a fight with a wild animal? Is that why you have that bare, red mark?"

Alaric looked down at the ground. "Oh, that is nothing important," he replied. "It is just the mark from my collar. It rubs my fur a little bit, but I do not mind."

"A collar?" Ulf asked, stopping in his tracks. "Why do you wear a collar? Does it mean you are tied up and cannot go wherever you want whenever you feel like moving around?"

"Well, yes," Alaric admitted. "During the day, they tie me to a post. I cannot run free, but they bring me food. At night, they let me out to guard the house."

Ulf backed away quickly. "No thank you!" he cried out. "I would rather be hungry and free than fat and chained. Keep your fancy scraps. I will keep my precious, wild freedom."

Ulf turned and ran back into the dark forest. He was still hungry, but his heart felt light and happy. He breathed the fresh air and enjoyed being his own master forever.

The moral is that freedom is more valuable than comfort. It is better to be poor and free than to live in luxury while being controlled by someone else. Choose your liberty.
