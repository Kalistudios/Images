The Farmer and the Stork

Silas the farmer felt very grumpy. Greedy cranes were eating all the freshly planted seeds in his golden field. He decided to set a large, strong net to catch the hungry thieves.

The next morning, Silas hurried back to his field. He saw many birds struggling under the heavy net. They had come to steal his grain, but now they were well and truly stuck.

Among the noisy, grey cranes was a gentle bird named Pinn. Pinn was a tall stork with white feathers. He had not come to steal, but he was trapped with the others.

Pinn flapped his long wings, trying desperately to escape the tangled mess. He did not belong here. He was a good bird who loved his parents and lived a very honest life.

Silas walked closer, looking very stern. He reached down to grab the net. The cranes chirped and fluttered in fear, knowing they had been caught red-handed stealing the farmer's precious corn seeds.

"Please, kind sir!" cried Pinn the stork. He looked up at Silas with big, sad eyes. "I am not a crane. I do not eat your seeds. Please let me go free!"

Pinn continued his plea. "I am a stork, a bird of excellent character. I look after my aging father and mother. I am not a thief like these naughty cranes beside me."

Silas listened to the stork's words. He looked at Pinn’s bright white feathers and his long, elegant legs. It was true that Pinn looked very different from the grey, seed-stealing cranes.

"Look at my feathers!" Pinn chirped hopefully. "They are not grey like theirs. I am helpful and kind. Surely you can see that I do not belong in a trap for thieves."

Silas shook his head slowly. He looked at the stork and then at the cranes. "Everything you say might be true," Silas replied. "You may be a very good bird, indeed."

"But," Silas added firmly, "I caught you with these thieves. You were flying with them and eating with them. Since you chose their company, you must now share their unlucky fate."

Pinn learned a sad lesson that day. It is not enough to be good; you must also choose your friends wisely. We are often judged by the company we keep.
