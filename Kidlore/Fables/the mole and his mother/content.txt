The Tale of Milo the Mole

Milo the mole lived in a cozy tunnel under the garden. He was very young and wanted to impress everyone. One morning, he made a very bold and big claim to his mother.

"Mother, I have wonderful news!" Milo shouted, waving his tiny paws. "I can see everything now! My eyes have opened, and I can see the whole world around our little home," he bragged.

Silty the mother mole looked at her son very carefully. She knew that moles were meant to dig, not to see. She wondered if Milo was just telling a very silly, make-believe story.

"Are you sure, Milo?" Silty asked kindly. "Our family has lived in the dark for a long time. It is rare for a mole to see as clearly as the bright, yellow sun."

Milo puffed out his chest and nodded his head quickly. "Yes, Mother! I can see the roots and the rocks. I am the first mole who can see perfectly in the dark."

Silty decided to test her son’s new skill. She wanted to see if he was telling the truth. She crawled away to find something special to help her with the clever test.

She found a small piece of frankincense. It was a special sap that had a very strong, sweet smell. She brought it back to where Milo was waiting in the dusty tunnel.

Silty placed the piece of frankincense on a flat stone. "If you can see so well, Milo, tell me what this object is," she said. She watched him with a small smile.

Milo squinted his tiny eyes and leaned forward. He wanted to look like he was seeing it. He did not want his mother to know that he was still mostly blind today.

"Oh, that is easy!" Milo bragged loudly. "I see it sitting right there on the stone. It is a pebble! A smooth, grey pebble that fell from the garden above our home."

Silty shook her head and sighed softly. "Oh, Milo," she said. "Not only can you not see, but you cannot even smell! That is a piece of sweet frankincense, not a pebble."

Milo’s face turned very red. He felt very silly for bragging about things he could not do. He realized that his mother knew the truth all along about his very poor eyes.

"I am sorry, Mother," Milo whispered. "I just wanted to be special. I wanted to do something that no other mole could do." He looked down at his big, strong digging paws.

Silty hugged Milo. "You are already special, little one. You are a great digger! You do not need to boast or tell stories to make me proud of you today," she said.

The moral of the story is to never brag about things you cannot do. Honesty is always better than boasting. Be proud of who you are and your own special talents.
