The Kindness of Small Friends

Pheidon the Ant was thirsty. He went to the sparkling river to drink. The water moved very fast, and suddenly, a large wave pulled him away from the safe, muddy riverbank.

Pheidon struggled in the cold, rushing water. He tried to swim, but he was too small and weak. He feared he would drown because no one could hear his tiny, quiet cries.

Up in a tall oak tree, Columba the Dove was resting. She looked down and saw the little ant spinning in the water. She felt very sad for the poor, drowning creature.

Columba wanted to help her tiny friend. She quickly plucked a large green leaf from a branch. She flew over the river, holding the leaf carefully in her bright, yellow beak.

Columba dropped the leaf right next to Pheidon. He grabbed onto the edge and climbed up. The leaf floated like a little boat, carrying him safely back toward the dry land.

Pheidon reached the shore and climbed onto the grass. He looked up at Columba and waved his tiny legs. He was very grateful that the kind bird had saved his life.

A few days later, Columba was sleeping on a low branch. She did not see a heavy net hanging above her. It was a dangerous trap hidden among the thick, green leaves.

The net began to fall slowly toward the sleeping bird. Columba was in great danger, but she was fast asleep. She did not know that she was about to be caught.

Pheidon was nearby, searching for crumbs. He saw the net moving and knew he had to act fast. He remembered how Columba had saved him from the deep, rushing river water.

Pheidon raced toward the rope that held the heavy net. He climbed up the rough bark of the tree. His tiny heart beat fast as he reached the thick, twisted cord.

Pheidon used his strong jaws to bite the rope. He chewed and chewed with all his might. He knew he had to break the cord before the net trapped his dear friend.

Suddenly, the rope snapped! The net fell away from Columba and landed on the ground with a loud thud. The noise woke the startled bird, and she flew up into the air.

Columba looked down and saw Pheidon on the branch. She realized that the tiny ant had saved her from the trap. She chirped a happy song to say a big thank you.

The two friends sat together in the warm sun. They knew that size did not matter when it came to kindness. Even the smallest friend can be a very big help.

The moral of this story is that one good turn deserves another. When you help someone in need, they will often find a way to help you in return later on.
