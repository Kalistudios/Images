The Horse and the Groom

Once, a wealthy man owned a magnificent Horse named Xanthus with a coat like silk. A Groom named Pilo was hired to care for him every day, ensuring he was the finest animal.

Every morning, Pilo spent many hours currying and brushing Xanthus's mane. He worked very hard to make sure the Horse's coat shone brightly under the warm and golden morning sunlight in the stable.

Xanthus stood patiently while Pilo combed his tail and polished his hooves. Though he looked beautiful on the outside, the Horse felt a deep and growing hunger inside his belly for some grain.

While Xanthus waited for his breakfast, Pilo did something very wicked. He secretly took the Horse’s daily measure of oats and corn, hiding the grain away in a heavy, brown burlap sack tonight.

Instead of feeding the hungry animal, Pilo took the stolen grain to the village market. He traded the Horse’s food for shiny silver coins, keeping the money for his own small wooden pocket.

Xanthus looked down at his empty trough with sadness. He watched Pilo return with empty hands, offering only more brushing instead of the nutritious meal the Horse so desperately needed to feel strong.

Days passed, and Xanthus began to grow quite thin and weak. His legs felt shaky, and he no longer had the energy to gallop across the green fields like he did before now.

Still, Pilo continued to brush Xanthus's coat until it sparkled brilliantly. He wanted everyone to think he was doing a wonderful job, even though he was starving the poor creature for more money.

One afternoon, Xanthus turned his head to look at Pilo. His ribs were starting to show through his shiny skin, and his eyes were dull from the lack of good food every day.

Pilo patted Xanthus's neck and bragged about how clean he looked. "You are the most beautiful horse in the land," Pilo said, while hiding more stolen grain behind his back in the stable.

Finally, Xanthus could stay silent no longer. He sighed a heavy sigh and spoke to Pilo. "If you really wish for me to look well, please listen to me now," the Horse said.

"You curry me and comb me every single day," said Xanthus. "But if you truly want me to be beautiful, stop the brushing and give me back my daily measure of golden corn."

Pilo stood frozen in surprise as Xanthus revealed his secret. Honesty is always better than greed, and the Horse knew that true health comes from nourishment, not just a shine on his back.

The moral of this story is that fine words and fancy appearances cannot replace true care. Being honest and providing what is necessary is more important than making things look good superficially to others.

Another lesson is that greed leads to harm. If you take what belongs to others for your own gain, you will eventually be found out and lose the trust of those around you today.
