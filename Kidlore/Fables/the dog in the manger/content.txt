The Dog in the Manger

Barken the dog was very tired after a long day of running. He searched for a quiet, cozy spot to take a long nap where no one would ever disturb his peaceful rest now.

He wandered into the old, wooden barn. It smelled of sweet grass and dry earth. In the corner, he saw a wooden manger filled with soft, yellow hay that looked perfect for sleeping.

Barken took a great leap and landed right in the middle of the manger. The hay crunched under his paws, but it felt much softer than the hard, cold ground outside the barn.

He circled around three times, making a perfect little nest for himself. Barken let out a happy sigh, tucked his tail close, and rested his chin upon the golden, dry stalks of hay.

Soon, Barken was fast asleep, dreaming of chasing bright butterflies. He did not hear the heavy footsteps approaching the barn. He was very comfortable and did not want to move from his spot.

Strawley the ox walked slowly into the barn. He had spent the whole day pulling a heavy plow through the fields. He was very exhausted and his stomach was growling loudly for food.

Strawley walked straight toward his manger. He was looking forward to a delicious meal of sweet hay before he went to sleep. The hungry ox could almost taste the dry, golden grass already.

As Strawley reached his head down to take a big bite, he saw the small dog. Barken woke up suddenly and looked very cross. He did not like being woken from his nap.

Barken stood up on the hay and began to bark loudly at Strawley. He bared his teeth and growled, warning the large ox to stay away from the manger and the soft hay.

Strawley backed away, feeling very surprised. He was much bigger than Barken, but he was a gentle animal. He just wanted to eat his dinner so he could finally rest his tired body.

"Please, Barken," Strawley said kindly. "I am very hungry and this hay is my food. You cannot eat hay, so please let me have my dinner now. I really need to eat today."

Barken did not care about Strawley’s hunger. He snapped at the ox's nose and barked even louder. He was determined to keep the manger all to himself, even if he did not need.

Strawley tried once more to reach the hay, but Barken jumped forward and snarled fiercely. The dog would not let the ox touch a single piece of the hay in the wooden manger.

The tired ox sighed and walked away with a very heavy heart. He was still hungry and sad. Barken lay back down, feeling proud of himself for guarding hay he could not eat.

Moral: Do not be selfish and begrudge others what you cannot enjoy yourself. It is very unkind to prevent someone from having something that is of no use to you at all.
