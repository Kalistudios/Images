The Fisherman and His Flute

Arion was a fisherman who loved music more than fishing. He believed his songs could charm the world. One sunny morning, he took his wooden flute down to the very edge of the deep sea.

He sat upon a smooth, grey rock overlooking the sparkling blue water. Arion was certain that the fish would hear his sweet melodies and hop right out of the waves to join him right now.

Arion began to play a bright, happy tune. The music drifted over the surface of the ocean. He watched the water closely, waiting for the first fish to leap up and start dancing today.

Deep below the waves, Marinus the fish watched the surface. He heard the strange music floating down. He felt the vibrations but stayed very still, hiding near the colorful, swaying green sea plants now.

Arion played a faster song, tapping his foot against the stone. He hoped the rhythmic beat would make the fish jump for joy and land inside the large fishing net lying beside his feet.

Marinus looked up at the shadow of the net above him. He was a very clever fish who knew better than to be tricked by a song. He stayed deep in the cool water.

Arion felt very disappointed. His music was beautiful, but the sea remained quiet and calm. Not a single fish had come out to dance. He realized his plan was not working at all today.

Arion sighed and set his flute down on the rock. He realized that music was for enjoyment, but fishing required a different tool. He reached for his wide, sturdy net instead of using music.

With a strong, practiced swing, Arion threw the net far out into the ocean. It opened like a giant spiderweb and sank slowly through the water, drifting down toward the sandy deep sea floor.

The net landed right over Marinus and his many friends. They were trapped! Marinus tried to swim away, but the net was very strong. He was pulled up toward the bright, sunny water surface.

Arion hauled the full net onto the hot, dry, warm sand. As soon as the little fish left the water, they began to wiggle, flap, and jump around in a very frantic, messy way.

Marinus flipped his tail and bounced high into the air. He was hopping and twitching with the other fish. To Arion, it looked like they were now performing a very energetic dance together today.

Arion looked down at the pile of gasping, jumping fish. He remembered how he had played his flute for them earlier. He stood with his hands on his hips and watched them dance away.

"You silly fish!" Arion cried out. "When I played my flute, you refused to dance for me. But now that I have stopped playing my music, you are dancing more than ever before now!"

Arion learned a lesson that day. Some things must be done in their own time. The moral is: There is a time for everything, so do the right thing at the right time always.
