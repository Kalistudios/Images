Patty and the Spilled Dreams

Patty was a happy milkmaid who lived on a farm. One sunny morning, she balanced a heavy pail of fresh, creamy milk on her head. She moved very carefully with every slow step.

With the milk balanced perfectly, Patty started her long walk to the busy market in town. The path was dusty, but she walked gracefully, keeping her back straight and her eyes looking ahead.

As she walked, Patty began to think about the coins she would receive for the milk. She could almost hear the clinking of the gold and silver money inside her small, empty pocket.

"I know what I shall do with my earnings," Patty whispered to herself. "I will go to the neighbor and buy a hundred of the finest, freshest eggs to take back home."

Patty imagined the eggs tucked warmly in a coop. Soon, they would crack open, and a hundred fluffy, yellow chicks would come out. She could see them hopping around her busy farmyard.

"When those chicks grow into fine chickens, I will sell them," she thought. "With that money, I will buy the most beautiful new dress to wear to the upcoming town fair."

Patty decided the dress must be bright green, just like the spring grass. It would have lovely silk ribbons and delicate lace. She would look like the most elegant lady in town.

At the fair, all the young men would see her in her green dress. They would walk over and ask Patty to dance. She imagined them standing in a long, hopeful line.

Patty felt very grand in her daydream. She decided she would be very picky. She would just toss her head back and say no to every single one of them with pride.

Patty became so excited by her own fancy thoughts that she forgot about the pail. She suddenly tossed her head back just as she had imagined doing to the boys at the fair.

Oh no! The heavy pail slipped right off her head. It crashed to the dusty ground, and all the cold, white milk splashed out and soaked into the dry, thirsty dirt path.

Patty stared at the empty pail. Gone were the eggs, the fluffy chicks, and the grown chickens. Gone was the beautiful green dress and the chance to dance at the town fair.

With tears streaming down her face, Patty picked up the empty pail and walked back home. She told her mother, Elspeth, all about her grand plans and the terrible, messy accident.

Elspeth hugged her daughter tightly. "Do not be so sad, Patty," she said softly. "But remember, it is never wise to count your chickens before they have actually hatched from their eggs."

Patty learned a very important lesson that day. It is wonderful to dream, but you must focus on the task at hand. Do not count your chickens before they are hatched!
