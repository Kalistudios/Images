The Crow and the Swan

Onyx the Crow was very proud of his clever mind. He spent his days flying over the green forest, searching for tasty treats and shiny treasures that humans often left behind on the dusty ground.

One day, Onyx saw Alabaster the Swan gliding gracefully across a crystal-clear lake. Her feathers were as white as a fluffy cloud in the blue sky. She looked so elegant and very purely beautiful there.

Onyx looked down at his own black feathers. They were as dark as the midnight sky. He began to feel very sad and jealous. He wished he could be as white as the lovely Alabaster.

"How are you so white, Alabaster?" Onyx asked with a deep sigh. "You must spend all your time in this magical water. Does the lake wash away all the dark colors from your soft wings?"

Alabaster tilted her long neck and looked at Onyx with kind eyes. "I simply live here," she replied softly. "The water is my home, but it does not change who I really am inside myself."

Onyx did not believe her. He thought that if he lived like a swan, he would surely turn white too. He decided to leave his forest home and stay by the big, deep blue lake.

Every morning, Onyx jumped into the cold water. He scrubbed his feathers with his beak, hoping to see a change. He washed himself many times, trying to scrub the blackness away from his wings completely.

Alabaster watched the poor crow from a safe distance. She saw him splashing and shivering in the waves. She felt worried for him because he was not meant to live in the deep, wet water.

Onyx was so busy washing that he forgot to eat his usual food. He could not find any grain or insects near the lake. He tried to eat the slimy, green weeds just like Alabaster.

The weeds did not taste good to Onyx. His tummy rumbled with hunger, and he felt very weak. Yet, he continued to scrub his dark feathers, waiting for the white color to appear very soon.

Days passed, and Onyx looked in the water's clear reflection. His feathers were still as black as coal. The water had not changed his color at all, no matter how much he washed himself daily.

Onyx sat on a rock, shivering and hungry. He realized that he could never be a white swan. He was a black crow, and no amount of washing would ever change his true, natural self.

Alabaster swam over to the tired crow. "You are a wonderful crow, Onyx," she said. "Your black feathers are beautiful in the sun. You should go home and be your happy, clever old self again."

Onyx flew back to his forest. He found plenty of food and felt strong once more. He finally understood that he was special just the way he was, even with his dark, shiny black feathers.

The moral of the story is that you cannot change your nature by changing your habits. Be proud of who you are, for everyone has their own unique beauty and special place in world.
