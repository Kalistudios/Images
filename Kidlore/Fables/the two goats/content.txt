The Narrow Bridge

Zenith lives on the North side of the mountain. He loves the high peaks and the thin air. He is a very stubborn goat who never likes to give up his path.

Kuno lives on the South side of the mountain. He is also very strong and very stubborn. He believes that every path he takes belongs only to him and nobody else.

Between their peaks lies a deep, dark canyon. A very narrow log bridge stretches across the rushing river far below. Zenith stood at the edge, looking across the wide, windy gap.

Zenith decided to cross the bridge to find some fresh grass. He stepped onto the narrow log with great confidence, feeling very proud of his strong, steady hooves and his sharp horns.

At that exact same moment, Kuno decided to cross the bridge from the other side. He wanted to see the flowers on the North peak. He stepped onto the narrow log bridge.

The two goats met right in the middle of the bridge. The log creaked and swayed over the deep canyon. Neither goat could move forward because the other goat was blocking them.

"Step aside and let me pass!" Zenith bleated loudly. He stomped his hoof on the wood. "I was here first, and I have very important business on the other side today."

Kuno shook his head and lowered his sharp horns. "No, you must step aside for me! I am much stronger than you, and I will not move backward for anyone today."

They stood nose to nose, glaring at each other with angry eyes. The river roared below them, but they were too stubborn to listen to the danger. Neither goat would move.

"If you do not move, I will push you off!" Zenith growled. He lunged forward, but Kuno stayed firm. The bridge shook violently as the two goats began to push and shove.

They locked their horns together with a loud crash. They pushed with all their might, trying to win the tiny path. Their hooves slipped on the wet, mossy bark of the log.

Suddenly, the narrow log bridge began to crack. The goats were so busy fighting that they did not notice the wood breaking. They both slipped and lost their balance very quickly.

With a loud splash, both Zenith and Kuno fell into the cold, rushing water below. They tumbled through the air, wishing they had been just a little bit more patient and kind.

They scrambled out of the river, soaking wet and very cold. They looked at each other and felt very foolish. Because they both refused to yield, neither goat reached the other side.

The moral of the story is: It is better to give way and be safe than to be stubborn and fall into ruin. Pride often leads to a very big fall.
