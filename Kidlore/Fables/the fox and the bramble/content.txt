The Fox and the Sharp Surprise

Silas the fox loved wandering through the dense, green forest. He was very clever and agile, jumping over logs and streams with great ease. The morning sun made his fur shine brightly today.

One day, Silas found a tall wall in the woods. He decided to jump over it to see the garden on the other side. He took a long, fast run toward the wall.

Silas jumped high, but his back paws slipped on the cold, wet stones. He lost his balance and started to tumble toward the hard ground. He felt very scared and quite helpless then.

As he fell, Silas saw a thick bush growing near the wall. He reached out with his paws, hoping to catch something that would stop his dangerous fall and keep him safe now.

He grabbed onto a sturdy branch belonging to Rubus the Bramble. Silas held on tight, thinking he was finally safe from the fall. But then, he felt a sharp and sudden stinging pain.

The sharp thorns of Rubus the Bramble dug deep into Silas's soft paws. The bush did not help him at all. Instead, it made his situation much worse than it was before today.

Silas let go of the bush and landed on the grass. He looked at his bleeding paws with sad eyes. He could not believe that the plant he trusted had hurt him so.

"You are a mean plant!" Silas shouted at the bush. "I reached out to you for help, and you did nothing but prick me with your sharp thorns. You are very unkind, Rubus."

Rubus the Bramble shook his green leaves and looked at the angry fox. He was not upset by the words Silas said. He stood tall and firm in the light of the sun.

"You were foolish to grab me, Silas," Rubus replied calmly. "My whole nature is to catch and hold onto everything that comes near me. I cannot change who I am for you."

Silas realized that he should have looked closer before grabbing the bush. He learned that some things are naturally hurtful, and we should not expect them to help us in our times.

Moral: It is foolish to seek help from those who are naturally harmful. Always choose your friends and your helpers wisely, for not everyone is meant to provide safety or kind comfort.
