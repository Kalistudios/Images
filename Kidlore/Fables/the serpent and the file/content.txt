The Serpent and the File

Sibil the snake slithered into the quiet workshop. It was very dark and cold. He was looking for something tasty to eat. His belly was empty, and he felt very, very hungry tonight.

Sibil looked around the wooden shelves. He saw many tools made of heavy metal. He did not see any mice or birds. He flicked his long tongue to find a sweet, yummy scent.

Then, Sibil spotted a long, gray object on the floor. It was Ferrum the iron file. Ferrum was very hard and very sharp. Sibil thought Ferrum looked like a delicious, silver fish.

"I have found my dinner!" Sibil hissed happily. He coiled his long body around the cold tool. Ferrum did not move at all. He just lay there, waiting in the dim moonlight.

Sibil opened his mouth wide. He took a very big bite of the iron file. CRUNCH! Sibil did not taste meat. He only felt the hard, rough surface of the strong metal.

Sibil was very surprised. His jaw felt a little bit sore. But Sibil was a very stubborn snake. He did not want to give up on his meal. He squeezed Ferrum tighter.

"You are a tough one!" Sibil cried out. He bit down again with all his might. But Ferrum was made of steel. He was much stronger than any snake in the whole world.

Sibil’s mouth began to hurt very much. He looked at Ferrum. The file did not have a single scratch. But poor Sibil’s teeth were starting to feel very blunt and very broken.

Ferrum finally spoke with a cold, metallic voice. "Why do you attack me, little snake? I am a file. I bite through iron! You are only hurting yourself by trying to bite me."

Sibil stopped biting. He looked at his own reflection in a puddle. His mouth was very red and sore. He realized that Ferrum was right. Not everything is meant to be eaten.

Sibil let go of the file and slithered away. He learned that some things are too tough to overcome. It is better to walk away than to keep hurting yourself for nothing.

Sibil found a soft egg in the grass instead. He was happy and safe now. Moral: Do not try to hurt those who are much stronger than you. Hard things can break you.
