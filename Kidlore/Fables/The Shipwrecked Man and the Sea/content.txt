The Sailor and the Sea

Aris the sailor loves the ocean. He sails his sturdy boat across the blue waves. The sun is shining, and the water is very calm. He feels very happy and safe today.

Suddenly, dark clouds gather in the sky. The wind begins to howl loudly. Big waves start to crash against Aris’s small boat. He tries to steer back toward the distant, sandy shore.

The storm grows stronger and very wild. A giant wave hits the boat. The wood breaks apart, and Aris falls into the cold, deep, and salty water. He is very scared.

Aris swims hard toward the distant land. He is tired, but he does not give up. Finally, he reaches the soft sand and crawls out of the reach of the waves.

He sits on the beach and looks at his broken boat. Aris feels very angry. He shakes his fist at the water and yells, "You are mean and very cruel, Sea!"

"You look so peaceful," Aris cries out. "But you hide dangerous traps for sailors like me. You take our boats and leave us with nothing. I will never trust you again."

Suddenly, the water begins to swirl and glow. A beautiful woman made of shimmering blue mist rises from the waves. This is Azura, the powerful and magical spirit of the deep sea.

Azura looks at Aris with calm eyes. Her dress is made of foam and seaweed. She stands tall on the water’s surface. She hears his angry words and wants to speak.

"Do not blame me, brave sailor," Azura says softly. Her voice sounds like the gentle lapping of waves on the sand. "I am not the one who caused your terrible shipwreck."

Aris looks up at Azura in surprise. He is confused by her words. "But it was your waves that broke my boat," he says. "How can you say you are innocent?"

Azura points to the sky where the wind is still blowing. "Look at the air," she explains. "I am naturally calm and still. It is the wind that makes me rough."

"The wind pushes me and stirs my depths," Azura continues. "Without the wind, I am like a giant, smooth mirror. I do not wish to hurt anyone who sails upon me."

Aris thinks about what Azura said. He remembers how peaceful the water was before the storm arrived. He realizes that the sea was only reacting to the power of the wind.

He stands up and bows to Azura. "I understand now," Aris says. "I was wrong to blame you for the storm. We must respect all the forces of the wild nature."

The moral is that we should not blame those who are forced to act by others. Sometimes, the real cause of a problem is hidden behind what we see at first.
