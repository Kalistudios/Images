The Eagle and the Golden Arrow

Aquilo was a majestic eagle with golden wings that shimmered under the bright sun. He loved to fly higher than any other bird, soaring through the fluffy white clouds in the big blue sky.

One breezy morning, Aquilo perched on a tall, jagged mountain peak to rest. As he carefully preened his beautiful wings, a single, perfect golden feather broke loose and floated gently down to earth.

Below in the valley, a clever hunter named Elian was walking through the woods. He spotted the golden feather shining in the grass. He picked it up and smiled, feeling very lucky today.

Elian sat on a mossy log and carefully tied the eagle’s feather to the back of his best arrow. He knew this strong feather would make his arrow fly straighter than any other.

Meanwhile, high above the trees, Aquilo was feeling very proud of himself. He circled the mountain peak, looking down at the world. He believed that nothing could ever reach him way up there.

Elian looked up and saw a golden speck moving across the bright sky. He wanted to test his new arrow. He pulled back his bowstring and aimed toward the very high mountain peak.

With a sharp WHOOSH, Elian released the arrow. It zoomed upward, climbing higher and higher into the sky. The golden feather on the end helped the arrow stay perfectly steady and very fast.

The arrow zipped past Aquilo, catching in the edge of his wing. It did not hurt him, but it made the big eagle tumble down from the sky toward a soft, yellow haystack.

Aquilo landed with a quiet thump. He was safe, but he could not fly back up to his mountain home. He looked closely at the wooden arrow that had brought him down today.

His eyes grew wide with surprise. Attached to the end of the arrow was his very own golden feather. He recognized the shape and the way it shimmered in the soft afternoon light.

Elian ran over to the haystack and saw the majestic bird. He realized the eagle was the source of his luck. He felt very bad for taking the bird away from the sky.

Elian helped Aquilo fly again. The moral is: We often provide the means for our own undoing. Always be careful with what you leave behind, for it might come back to you.
