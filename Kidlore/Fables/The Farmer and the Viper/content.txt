The Farmer and the Winter Viper

Silas was a very kind man who lived on a small farm. He loved every creature, big and small. He always worked hard and shared his food with those who were hungry.

One morning, a great winter storm arrived. The sky turned gray and very cold. Silas walked through the deep snow. He wrapped his thick cloak tightly around his tall, strong and weary shoulders.

Near a frozen pond, Silas saw something dark in the snow. It was Vesper, a long viper. The snake was stiff and very cold. Vesper did not move even a little bit.

Silas knelt down in the soft snow. He looked at Vesper with sad eyes. He knew that the poor snake would surely die if he did not help it stay warm tonight.

Silas gently reached out and picked up the frozen snake. Vesper felt like a cold piece of ice in his warm hands. The viper was completely still and did not even hiss.

"I must help you," Silas whispered. He unbuttoned his heavy coat. He tucked Vesper deep inside, close to his own chest. He hoped the heat from his body would save the snake.

Silas walked back to his warm cottage. He could feel Vesper against his skin. The viper was slowly starting to thaw out. Silas smiled because he thought he was doing something good.

Inside his home, a bright fire crackled. Silas sat by the hearth. He carefully took Vesper out of his coat. He laid the long snake on the rug near the golden flames.

Vesper began to wiggle. His green scales started to shine in the light. He was warm now. He stretched his long body and opened his yellow eyes to look at the room.

Silas reached out to pat the snake. Suddenly, Vesper coiled up. He did not feel thankful at all. Instead, he bared his sharp fangs and lunged at the kind farmer's helping hand.

Vesper bit Silas and then slithered away quickly. Silas cried out in pain. He realized that a wicked creature will never change its nature, no matter how much kindness you show it today.

The moral is that kindness is wasted on the wicked. You should be careful who you help. Some will only hurt you in return, even if you save their life from death.
