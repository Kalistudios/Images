The King of the Pond

In a big, bubbly pond, lived many happy frogs. They swam in the blue water and hopped on green pads. Life was very easy, but the frogs felt something was missing from home.

"We need a leader!" shouted Puddles to the group. "A king would make us feel very important and grand." Gulp agreed, nodding his head. They both wanted someone to rule over their quiet pond.

The frogs looked up at the bright, blue sky. They called out to the Great Sky King. "Please send us a powerful ruler!" they cried. They waited for a sign from the clouds.

The Sky King laughed at the silly frogs. He picked up a heavy, old log and tossed it down. The log fell through the air, heading straight for the middle of the pond.

SPLASH! The log hit the water with a very loud noise. Big waves pushed Puddles and Gulp away. All the frogs dove deep under the lily pads, hiding from the giant new king.

For a long time, the pond was very quiet. Puddles peeked out from behind a rock. He saw the big, brown King Log floating still. It did not move or say a single word.

Gulp swam closer to the log very slowly. He poked it with his little frog hand. The log just bobbed in the water. "Is this our king?" asked Gulp. "He seems very, very quiet."

Soon, the frogs grew very brave. They hopped onto King Log and began to dance. They realized the log could not do anything. They began to laugh at their lazy and very wooden king.

"This king is boring!" Puddles shouted at the sky. "We want a king who does things! Send us a real king who is strong and fast!" The frogs were not happy with peace.

The Sky King grew angry with the frogs. "You do not like your quiet king?" he asked. "Fine! I will send you a king who is very busy and very active indeed today."

A large shadow fell over the pond. A tall bird named Longbeak landed on the shore. He had very long legs and a sharp beak. The frogs cheered for their new, grand king.

Longbeak did not waste any time. He stepped into the water with a hungry look. He snatched up Gulp in his long beak. The frogs realized their new king was very, very dangerous.

Puddles tried to hide under a big green leaf. "Help!" he cried. "This king is eating us! We liked King Log much better!" But it was too late to change their minds now.

The pond was no longer a happy place. The frogs were always running and hiding from Longbeak. They missed the days when they were free to swim and play in the sunny water.

The moral of the story is: Be happy with what you have. It is much better to have a quiet leader who does no harm than a powerful one who treats you badly.
