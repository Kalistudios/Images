The Soot and the Silk

Ember worked hard every single day. He made black charcoal from heavy wood in the deep, dark forest. He was covered in black dust from his messy head to his dirty toes.

Ember lived all alone in a tiny wooden house. "Life is getting very expensive," he sighed softly. "I should find a good friend to share my home and pay half the bills."

Near the sparkly blue river lived a busy man named Suds. Suds was a fuller who spent his long days washing heavy cloth until it looked as white as the fluffy clouds.

Suds worked very hard to keep everything around him perfectly tidy. He scrubbed and rubbed the fabrics in big wooden tubs until they glowed. He was very proud of his spotless, clean shop.

One sunny morning, Ember saw Suds hanging laundry outside. Ember thought Suds looked like a very nice person. "He would be the perfect roommate for me," Ember whispered with a happy grin.

Ember walked over to the river to meet Suds. He left little black footprints on the green grass. Suds looked up from his work and saw the dark, soot-covered man approaching him.

"Good morning, friend!" Ember called out loudly. "I have a wonderful plan for us. We should live together in my house. It would be much cheaper for both of us to share."

Ember explained that they could share the food and the rent. He smiled brightly, but his face was sooty. He really wanted a friend to talk to at the end of day.

Suds listened quietly to the proposal. He looked at Ember’s dark hands and then at his own white cloth. He thought about the dusty charcoal and the clean, bright water he used.

Ember pointed toward his small cottage. "Come see where I live!" he invited. "It is just over there." The cottage was surrounded by piles of black coal and clouds of dark, smoky dust.

Suds walked to the door and peeked inside the house. He saw black dust on the floor, the chairs, and the table. Everything Ember touched turned black instantly. Suds shook his head.

"I am sorry, Ember," Suds said gently. "Your plan sounds very smart for saving money, but it would never work for me. My job is to make things clean and very white."

Suds explained the problem. "If I moved into your house, your charcoal dust would get on my fabrics. Whatever I wash and make clean, you would immediately make black and very dirty."

Ember understood that Suds was right. Their jobs were too different to be under one roof. They decided to stay neighbors instead. They remained friends, but they lived in their own houses.

Like will draw like. It is very important to choose your companions wisely. Ensure your way of life matches those you live with. Some things, like soot and white cloth, cannot mix.
