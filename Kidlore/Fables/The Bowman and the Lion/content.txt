The Hunter and the Lion

Arken the hunter goes to the high mountains. He carries a bow and a quiver full of arrows. The sun shines brightly over the rocky peaks as he looks for a challenge today.

Deep in the forest, Tawny the Lion hears footsteps. He is the king of the woods and fears no one. He shakes his golden mane and gives a very loud, mighty roar.

Arken walks past the bushes and trees. He is very brave and strong. He knows that a great beast lives nearby. He keeps his hand close to his trusty, long wooden bow.

Arken sees the great Lion standing on a tall rock. He does not run away in fear. Instead, he reaches into his bag and pulls out a long, sharp, wooden arrow very slowly.

Tawny leaps down from the rock and growls. He thinks he can scare the hunter easily. “Do not come closer,” Arken says quietly. He fits the arrow to his strong, flexible bow.

The Lion laughs a deep, rumbling laugh. He thinks he is much faster than any tiny stick. He prepares to spring forward and catch Arken with his big, sharp and heavy claws.

Arken pulls back the string of his bow. His eyes are focused and steady. He breathes out slowly. The wind whispers through the green trees as he aims for the proud, golden Lion.

Suddenly, the arrow flies through the air like a fast bird. It moves quicker than the eye can see. Tawny tries to jump away, but the arrow is just a bit too fast.

The arrow hits the Lion’s shoulder with a sharp sting. Tawny yelps in surprise and pain. He has never been hurt from such a far distance before. He feels very afraid now.

Arken stands tall and watches the Lion. He does not shoot another arrow. He wants the Lion to learn a lesson about strength and speed. He holds his bow ready just in case.

Tawny turns around and starts to run away into the dark woods. He does not look back. He is fast, but the sting of the arrow is much faster and more painful.

Tawny stops running once he is far away. He looks at the small wound and sighs. He realizes that he was not as safe as he thought he was in his own mountains.

Arken puts his bow away and walks back home. He is happy that he did not have to fight more. He knows his skill is his best defense against the big, wild world.

From that day on, Tawny the Lion is much more careful. He knows that some enemies can reach him even if they are standing very far away. He respects the clever hunter.

The moral of the story is that we should always be careful of those who can strike from a distance. A clever mind is often much more powerful than just big muscles.
