The Color of the Heart

Silas meets Kofi at the busy market. Kofi is tall and strong, with skin like polished mahogany. Silas thinks he will be a wonderful helper for his big house and beautiful gardens.

Silas brings Kofi home to his bright villa. He looks closely at Kofi’s dark skin and frowns. Silas wrongly believes that Kofi is only dark because he was never cleaned by anyone.

"I shall make you shine!" Silas exclaims loudly. He believes that his previous master must have been very lazy. Silas gathers many bars of soap and several very large, heavy metal buckets.

Silas fills a large wooden tub with steaming water. He adds special salts and a mountain of white bubbles. Kofi watches with a puzzled look, wondering what Silas is planning to do.

Kofi climbs into the warm tub slowly. Silas rolls up his sleeves and grabs a thick sponge. He is ready to scrub away what he thinks is just layers of garden dust.

Silas begins to scrub Kofi’s arms with great energy. He expects to see pale skin appearing underneath. Kofi sits patiently, hoping the bath will be over very quickly and easily for him.

The water becomes gray and murky from the soap. However, Kofi’s skin remains the same deep color. Silas decides he must use a much harder brush to get the results he wants.

Silas scrubs harder, his face turning quite red. Kofi begins to feel very sore and cold. The sun starts to set, and a chilly breeze blows into the tiled bathing room now.

For many hours, Silas does not stop his work. He uses every soap he owns, but nothing changes. Kofi’s skin is naturally dark and beautiful, just like the starry night sky.

Poor Kofi begins to shiver and sneeze. The long bath and the harsh scrubbing are making him feel sick. His eyes grow heavy, and his muscles feel very tired and very weak.

Silas finally notices that Kofi is shaking. He looks at his own red hands and then at Kofi’s unchanged skin. He realizes his plan has failed completely and utterly in the end.

Kofi climbs out of the tub, feeling very weak. He is shivering from the cold water and the wind. Silas feels a deep pang of guilt in his heavy heart now.

Silas wraps Kofi in a thick, woolly blanket. He gives him hot tea to drink. Silas finally understands that he cannot change what nature has created so perfectly and so beautifully.

Kofi recovers and smiles at Silas again. Silas promises never to try and change him. He learns to appreciate Kofi’s strength and his kind, gentle heart every single day they share.

The moral is that nature's colors are permanent. We should never try to change someone’s appearance. True friendship begins with accepting others exactly as they are, with love and much respect.
