The Owl and the Grasshopper

Glaux the Owl tries to sleep in an old oak tree. The sun is bright and the beautiful forest is quiet. She tucks her head under her wing and starts to dream.

Suddenly, a loud noise woke Glaux. Tettix the Grasshopper was sitting on a nearby leaf. He began to sing a very noisy song. His chirping echoed through the warm, sunny, peaceful woods.

Glaux looked out from her dark hollow. "Please stop singing, Tettix," she requested politely. "I sleep during the day and hunt at night. Your loud music is making me feel very tired."

Tettix just laughed at the old owl. "I have every right to sing in the sun," he chirped loudly. He began to jump and dance on the green leaves, singing even louder.

Glaux grew very angry. She wanted to snap at the rude grasshopper, but her eyes were weak in the bright sunlight. She could not see well enough to catch the small insect.

The clever owl thought of a sneaky plan. She changed her tone and spoke kindly to Tettix. "Your voice is actually quite beautiful," she said with a very big, friendly owl smile.

Tettix stopped jumping and looked up. He was very surprised to hear such nice words. "Do you really think so?" he asked. He felt very proud of his loud and noisy song.

"Oh, yes!" Glaux replied warmly. "Your music sounds like a wonderful harp. I have some delicious nectar sent from the gods. Please, come up to my hollow and share it with me."

Glaux continued her flattery. "We can sit together in the cool shade," she suggested. "I would love to hear your glorious songs up close while we sip this sweet and tasty drink."

Tettix was very thirsty and very vain. He believed every single word the owl said. He forgot that owls and grasshoppers are not usually friends. He wanted to hear more sweet praise.

Tettix took a giant leap from his leaf. He landed right on the edge of the owl's dark hollow. He looked inside, searching for the sweet nectar the owl had promised him.

As soon as Tettix was close enough, Glaux pounced. She was no longer sleepy or kind. She caught the foolish grasshopper in her sharp beak before he could even say a word.

Glaux finished her meal and felt much better. The forest was finally quiet again. She tucked her wings in and went back to sleep in her dark, cozy home in the tree.

The noisy grasshopper was gone because he had listened to the owl's fake praise. He let his pride get in the way of his safety. Glaux slept soundly until the bright moon rose.

The moral is that flattery is often a trap. Do not trust those who praise you only to get what they want. Stay wise and be careful of too many kind words.
