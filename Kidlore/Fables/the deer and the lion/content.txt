The Crown and the Spindly Legs

Cervos the deer was thirsty. He found a clear, blue pool in the deep woods. The sun was warm, and the forest was very quiet. He leaned down to take a drink.

Cervos saw his reflection in the still water. He noticed his grand antlers. They were tall and wide like branches. "I am so handsome!" he whispered to himself. He felt very proud.

Then, Cervos looked down at his legs. He saw they were thin and spindly. He frowned at his reflection. "My legs are so ugly and weak," he sighed. "They do not match me."

Cervos stood by the water for a long time. He kept admiring his crown of horns and hating his thin legs. He wished he had thick, strong limbs like the big, powerful bears.

Suddenly, a loud roar echoed through the trees. Ari the lion stepped out from the bushes. Ari was hungry and looking for a meal. Cervos felt a sudden wave of great fear.

Ari the lion crouched low, preparing to spring forward. His golden eyes were fixed on the deer. Cervos knew he had to move fast. He turned and started to run away quickly.

Cervos's thin legs moved with amazing speed. He dashed across the open field. His legs, which he hated, were now saving his life. Ari the lion followed close behind, growling very loudly.

The race went into the thick forest. Cervos was still ahead because his legs were so light. But then, he ran into a patch of low trees with many tangling, sharp branches.

Suddenly, Cervos's beautiful antlers got caught in the branches. He pulled and pulled, but he was stuck. He felt his heart beating fast as he heard the heavy paws of the lion approaching.

Ari the lion was almost there, showing his sharp teeth. Cervos gave one final, desperate tug. His antlers snapped free of the wood. He was loose and ready to run once more.

Cervos dashed away on his thin legs. He left the forest and the lion far behind. He finally stopped to rest under a tall tree, breathing heavily but feeling very safe again.

Moral: We often value what is pretty but useless and dislike what is helpful. True worth is found in what helps us most, not in what looks the best to the eyes.
