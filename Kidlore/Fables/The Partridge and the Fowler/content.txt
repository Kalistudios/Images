The Partridge and the Fowler

Silas the clever fowler set his large net in the green woods. He hoped to catch many birds for his dinner that day.

Pip the beautiful partridge flew down and got caught in the net. She fluttered her wings but she could not get away today.

Silas came to the net and grabbed the poor bird. Pip was very scared and she began to beg him for her life.

"Please let me go," Pip cried out. "I will help you catch many more birds if you set me free right now," she said.

Pip promised to call her friends into the net. She would trick them so Silas could catch them all for his dinner tonight.

Silas looked at the bird and shook his head. He was not happy with the mean and tricky offer that Pip made him.

"I was going to kill you for my meal," said Silas. "But now I have an even better reason to do it today."

"You are willing to betray your own friends to save yourself," Silas said. "That is a very wicked thing to do to them."

Pip felt very bad for what she had said to Silas. She knew that she was being a very bad friend indeed today.

Silas held the bird very tightly and did not let go. He did not like anyone who was not loyal to their friends.

"A person who betrays their own kind is not to be trusted," Silas explained as he looked at the bird in his hands.

Pip realized that her plan had failed. Trying to be mean to others did not help her stay safe in the big woods.

Silas took the bird home. He knew that honesty and loyalty were much more important than a tricky, mean deal with a bird.

The story of Pip reminds us all to be kind. Never try to hurt your friends just to help yourself out of trouble.

Moral: He who betrays his friends to save himself will often find that he has lost everything in the end of the day.
