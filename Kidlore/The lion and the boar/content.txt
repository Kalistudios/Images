The Lion and the Boar

It was a sweltering afternoon in the dry, dusty savannah. The sun beat down, making every creature feel very thirsty. Jabari the Lion wandered slowly, his heavy paws kicking up orange dust as he searched for a cool drink of water.

After walking for hours, Jabari finally discovered a tiny, bubbling spring hidden between some ancient rocks. The water was clear and cold, reflecting the bright sky. He prepared to take a deep sip, his parched throat aching for relief.

At that very same moment, a sturdy wild boar named Ngiri emerged from the thick, dry bushes. He was panting heavily from the heat and rushed toward the small spring, desperate for a refreshing drink to cool his tired body.

Jabari and Ngiri met right at the edge of the water. Both animals stopped suddenly and glared at one another. Neither wanted to step aside and let the other one drink the water first, as there was only room for one at a time.

"Step back!" roared Jabari, baring his sharp teeth and making the ground shake. "I am the King, so I shall drink first." Ngiri snorted loudly, a puff of dust escaping his snout, and lowered his tusks, refusing to move even an inch away.

The argument quickly turned into a fierce battle. Jabari pounced with his large paws, and Ngiri charged forward with his strong shoulders. They fought with all their might, kicking up thick clouds of yellow dust near the quiet, little water spring.

They tumbled across the dry grass, biting and scratching as they rolled. Each animal was determined to win the right to drink first. The intense heat of the afternoon sun made them even angrier as they struggled against each other fiercely.

Exhausted and out of breath, they both stopped to catch their breath. They stood several feet apart, panting heavily in the hot sun. Their hearts hammered against their chests as they stared at each other, preparing for the next round of their fight.

Suddenly, Jabari looked up into the bright blue sky. He saw a flock of vultures circling high above them, their dark wings spread wide. The birds were waiting patiently for one of the fighters to fall down and move no more.

"Look there," Jabari whispered, pointing his heavy, furry paw toward the hungry birds in the sky. Ngiri looked up and shivered despite the heat. He realized that the vultures were not just watching; they were waiting for a feast of their remains.

"Those birds want us to kill each other," Jabari said thoughtfully, his voice low and serious. "If we keep fighting, we will both end up as dinner for those scavengers. Tell me, is this tiny spring worth dying for?"

Ngiri the Boar nodded his head in slow agreement. "You are right, Lion," he said softly. "It is much better for us to be friends and share than to become food for the vultures. Let us stop this fighting right now."

They settled their dispute immediately. Jabari and Ngiri decided to share the cool water together. They stepped to the edge of the spring side by side, leaving their anger and their battle behind in the settling dust.

They drank deeply until their thirst was completely gone. The water tasted much better now that they were at peace and sharing. They looked at each other with newfound respect, glad they chose kindness and cooperation over war.

The moral of this story is that it is much better to settle our differences and be friends than to fight and let others profit from our foolish and dangerous anger.
