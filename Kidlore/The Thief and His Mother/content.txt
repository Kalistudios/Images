The Lesson of the First Stone

Once upon a time, a young boy named Silas sat in his bright classroom. He saw a beautiful, shiny book on the teacher’s wooden desk and wanted it for his very own collection.

While the teacher was busy, Silas grabbed the shiny book and hid it under his small tunic. He ran all the way home to find his mother, Lyra, and show her the prize.

Instead of scolding Silas, Lyra smiled and patted his head. She told him he was very clever for finding such a prize. Silas felt that stealing might actually be a good thing.

Years passed, and Silas grew into a tall teenager. He decided to take a fancy silver clock from the town square. He remembered how his mother had praised his cleverness long ago.

Lyra clapped her hands with joy when she saw the beautiful clock Silas brought home. She did not ask where it came from. She only said he was a successful young man.

Now Silas was a grown man. He tried to steal a heavy chest filled with gold from the palace. But this time, the guards were watching him closely from the tall shadows.

A guard grabbed Silas before he could escape with the gold. They tied his hands with thick rope. Silas felt very afraid as he was led away toward the dark town jail.

Lyra followed behind Silas, weeping loudly and waving her hands in the air. She could not believe her son was in trouble. Silas looked back at her with a very sad face.

"Please let me speak to my mother one last time," Silas begged the guard. The guard agreed and allowed Lyra to step closer to her son so he could whisper in her ear.

As Lyra leaned in, Silas looked at her sternly. He told her that if she had corrected him when he stole the book, he would not be in this deep trouble today.

Lyra realized her great mistake. She had not taught Silas the difference between right and wrong. She cried bitter tears as the guard finally led her son away into the cold night.

The moral of the story is that we must correct small faults early. If we do not learn what is right as children, our mistakes will only grow bigger as we age.
