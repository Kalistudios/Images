The Miller's Lesson

Silas was a jolly miller with a bright red hat. He lived in a windmill with his young son, Milo. One morning, they decided to sell their donkey, Barnaby, at the market.

Silas and Milo started their long walk. Barnaby trotted happily beside them. They did not ride him because they wanted him to look fresh and strong for the busy buyers at the market.

Soon, they passed a group of laughing girls. "Look at those silly people!" one cried. "They are walking when they have a perfectly good donkey to ride. How very foolish they both are!"

Silas did not want to be foolish. He told Milo to climb onto Barnaby’s back. Milo smiled and hopped up. Now, the boy was riding while his father walked slowly along the road.

They met some old men resting under a shady tree. One man grumbled, "Look at that lazy boy! He rides comfortably while his poor, old father must walk on his tired, aching feet."

Milo felt very bad hearing this. He quickly hopped down and told Silas to ride instead. Silas climbed up, and Milo walked happily behind. They thought they had finally found the right way.

Next, they passed three women carrying heavy baskets. "Shame on that man!" one shouted. "He sits like a king while his small son can barely keep up. What a selfish, lazy father he is!"

Silas wanted to be a good father. He pulled Milo up behind him. Now, both Silas and Milo were riding Barnaby together. They felt sure that no one could find any fault now.

A townsman stopped them near the bridge. "You cruel people!" he yelled. "That poor donkey is far too small to carry both of you. You are heavy, and he is looking very tired!"

Silas and Milo were very confused now. "What should we do?" asked Milo. They decided the only way to please everyone was to carry Barnaby themselves. They found a long, sturdy wooden pole.

They tied Barnaby’s feet to the pole. It was a very strange sight indeed. Silas took one end of the pole. He hoisted his end of the heavy donkey up onto his shoulder.

Milo took the other end of the pole. He struggled to keep it steady. The bridge was very high. Barnaby became very frightened and started to kick his legs in the air wildly.

Barnaby kicked his legs hard to get free. The rope snapped! He tumbled off the pole and fell into the deep river below. He swam away quickly, leaving the two men standing alone.

Silas and Milo watched Barnaby disappear. They had no donkey to sell and no money for the market. They walked home slowly, feeling very sad about trying to please every person they met.

The moral of the story is that if you try to please everyone, you will please no one. It is best to use your own heart and mind to make your own choices.
