Aristos and the Giant Neighbor

Aristos lived in a sparkling pond filled with green lily pads. He was a very proud frog who thought he was the most important creature in the entire world. He loved his home.

Every afternoon, Aristos told grand stories to his young son, Pippin. He claimed to be the strongest and bravest frog ever. Pippin listened with wide eyes, thinking his father was truly a giant.

One warm day, a massive ox named Asterion walked slowly toward the pond. He wanted a cool drink of water. His heavy hooves made the ground shake with every single giant step.

Pippin watched from behind a leaf as the huge beast lowered his head. He had never seen anything so big! The ox was like a mountain covered in soft, brown, fuzzy fur.

Pippin hopped back to Aristos. "Father! I just saw a monster!" he cried. "It was the biggest thing in the world! It was much, much larger than you are, dear Father," Pippin said.

Aristos frowned and narrowed his eyes. He did not like being smaller than anyone. "Was he really that big?" he asked. Aristos took a deep breath and puffed out his green chest.

"Was he as big as this?" Aristos asked proudly. Pippin shook his head quickly. "Oh no, Father! The beast was much bigger than that," the little frog replied, looking at the giant ox.

Aristos took an even bigger breath. His skin stretched tight like a balloon. He stood on his tiptoes and tried to look very tall. "How about now?" he croaked with a strain.

Asterion the ox continued to drink his water peacefully. He did not even notice the tiny frog nearby. He was huge and strong without even trying to be anything but himself today.

Pippin looked at his father, who was now very round. "You are still not big enough," Pippin whispered. Aristos was determined to be the biggest. He took one more giant, shaky breath.

Suddenly, Aristos felt very dizzy. He was so full of air that he could not speak! With a loud whoosh, he zoomed around the pond like a flat balloon, losing all air.

Aristos landed softly on a lily pad. He felt much better being small. "I am happy being a frog," he said. Pippin hugged him. Being yourself is better than being a giant.
