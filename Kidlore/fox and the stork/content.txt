The Lesson of the Tall Jar

Russet the Fox had a mischievous idea. He invited his friend Beakley the Stork to a fancy dinner. "Please come over tonight," Russet said with a grin. Beakley smiled and happily agreed to go.

Russet worked hard in his small kitchen. He cooked a tasty soup that smelled wonderful. He wanted to play a trick on Beakley, so he hummed a little song while he worked alone.

When Beakley arrived, Russet poured the soup onto two very flat, shallow plates. "Enjoy your dinner!" Russet said. He began to lap up the soup quickly with his tongue, feeling very proud and clever.

Poor Beakley tried to eat, but her long, thin beak could not pick up any soup. She tapped at the flat plate, but she stayed hungry while Russet finished every single delicious drop himself.

Beakley did not complain about the meal. She was very polite and thanked Russet for the invitation. "Please come to my house for dinner tomorrow," she said kindly before flying home under the stars.

The next day, Russet arrived at Beakley’s home. He was very hungry and his tummy made a loud grumbling sound. He expected another delicious meal and sat down at the wooden table with Beakley.

Beakley had cooked a savory stew with fish and herbs. She poured the food into two tall, narrow jars with very thin necks. The steam rose up, making the whole room smell wonderful.

Russet stared at the tall jar in front of him. He tried to lick the top, but his tongue could not reach the food. He felt his tummy growl even louder than before.

Beakley easily put her long beak into the tall jar. She enjoyed the tasty stew while Russet watched her. "Is your dinner good, Russet?" she asked. Russet just looked sadly at his jar.

Russet realized that Beakley was teaching him a lesson. He remembered the flat plates and felt very ashamed. He looked at Beakley and knew he had been a very unkind and selfish host.

"I am sorry for my trick," Russet whispered softly. He realized that if you want to have friends, you must treat them with respect. He promised himself to be much kinder from now on.

Beakley smiled and shared her food on better bowls. They ate together and laughed. Russet learned that being kind is better than playing tricks. They remained the best of friends in the forest.
