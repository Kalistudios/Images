The Fisher and the Little Fish

Elias was a very patient fisherman who lived in a small, wooden hut by the blue river. Every morning, he carried his heavy nets down to the sparkling water to start his very long day.

The sun was warm on his back as Elias stood on the rocky shore. He threw his large, brown net far into the water, hoping to catch many big fish for his dinner tonight.

Elias waited for a very long time. The clouds drifted slowly across the bright sky, but his net stayed empty. He began to feel a bit sad because he was getting very hungry now.

Finally, Elias felt a small tug on his rope. He pulled the net onto the grass with a big smile. Inside the giant net sat one tiny, shimmering fish named Finnegan, wiggling about joyfully.

Finnegan was a very small fish with bright, silver scales. He looked up at the giant fisherman with big, watery eyes. He was very scared and wanted to go back home to the deep river.

Elias picked up the tiny fish with his rough hands. He frowned because the fish was too small to make a meal. He looked at Finnegan and wondered if he should let him go free.

"Please let me go!" Finnegan cried out. "I am much too small to eat right now. If you put me back into the water, I will grow into a very, very big, fat fish."

The little fish wiggled more. "If you wait until next year, I will be a giant meal for you. You can catch me again when I am big and strong. Please let me go now."

Elias looked at the river and then back at Finnegan. He thought about the promise of a huge dinner in the future. He listened to the little fish’s tiny voice and he smiled very softly.

Elias shook his head. "I would be very silly to let you go," he said. "A tiny fish in my hand right now is better than a big fish that is not even caught yet."

Elias put Finnegan into his basket safely. He knew that one small fish was better than having no food at all. He walked home happily, satisfied with the little prize he had found today.

The moral is: A small gain is worth more than a large promise in the future. It is better to keep what you have than to risk it for something bigger that might never come.
